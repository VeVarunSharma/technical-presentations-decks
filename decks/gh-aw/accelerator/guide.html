Readiness summary

Use for evaluation and rehearsal; do not describe this package as production certified or catalog accepted. This guide documents the intended handoff, not evidence that every gate has passed.

The replay uses synthetic fixtures, not GitHub API calls or a model. A staged proposal is neither an applied repository change nor an accepted business outcome. The walkthrough records a demonstration, not a live GH-AW execution.

Customer story (illustrative)

A platform team has a maintenance queue but little confidence that another automated suggestion will deserve review. A maintainer wants evidence and duplicate checks before an agent proposes any work. The team first rehearses three synthetic cases: useful work, work already tracked, and insufficient evidence. Security and engineering then agree the boundary for a small staged pilot, with a named reviewer and an explicit stop rule. This is a scenario, not a customer testimonial or measured result.

Numbered customer journey

  1. Discover: identify one repository-care decision and its human owner. Complete the eight questions below before suggesting execution.
  2. Explain: open the deck at “Governed automation for the agentic software factory”; state the difference between static replay and optional execution.
  3. Inspect: use “The agent's job is written as an operating procedure” and repo-care.md to inspect intent, triggers, and bounds.
  4. Rehearse: open replay and compare the useful, duplicate, and insufficient-evidence fixture cases. Show their raw source in replay; do not represent fixture text as model reasoning or a run log.
  5. Decide: use “Earn the right to scale the process” to choose no-go, further discovery, or an approved sandbox pilot.
  6. Hand off: assign the operator, reviewer, cost owner, security approver, maintainer contact, and next review date in the decision record.

Presenter controls and fallback

In the deck, Space, Right Arrow, or Page Down reveals the next build before advancing. Left Arrow or Page Up reverses a build before moving back. Home resets builds and returns to slide 1; numbered dot navigation jumps directly to a slide. Use the visible Back/Hide and Next/Reveal buttons, Full screen (or f outside a control), and Sources. Touch navigation and print are provided by the shared runtime; browser print reveals build content. Do not install a second navigation implementation.

Rehearse in the delivery browser, including keyboard focus and reduced-motion settings. Use the labeled fixture controls in replay and inspect its raw source display. If replay or the network fails, open the transcript, fixture file, and local recording with captions. If media cannot play, narrate the transcript and source files. Never replace a failed replay with an unapproved live workflow run.

Architecture: two separate trust boundaries

The static host publishes files; it cannot establish execution permissions or prove controls in a customer's environment. Even staged execution can consume compute/model credits and process sensitive input. Inspect generated jobs, logs, artifacts, network destinations, and credential scope with the operator before any live run. Staging previews requested writes; it does not remove every execution or disclosure risk.

Do not enable or run the workflow in a production repository as part of this handoff. Use an isolated, organization-approved sandbox with synthetic issues and no production secrets, customers, deployment integrations, or sensitive data. What staging actually means

The supplied safe-outputs.staged: true previews requested writes rather than automatically applying them. Its proposed issue outputs use automation and needs-review labels, a maximum of two issues and title deduplication; proposed pull requests are draft with a maximum of one and protected-file fallback. Those source settings do not demonstrate that an issue or PR was created. Do not turn staging off for a demo. Human acceptance, any later write enablement, and production rollout are separate decisions.

Stop and cleanup plan

For an independently approved sandbox run, the operator must be able to pause the optional workflow in Actions, cancel a specific active run, and verify no queued or scheduled execution remains before closing the exercise. Follow approved credential expiry/revocation and artifact-retention procedures. Do not delete repositories, issues, branches, labels, environments, or customer data as a demo reset. If these controls cannot be established without production changes, use static replay only.

Discovery worksheet: eight questions

Discuss these before proposing a pilot. Record sanitized answers in the scorecard or a customer-approved system—not in public fixture data.

  1. Which specific maintenance decision consumes reviewer time, and what is the current workflow?
  2. Which repository, owner, and low-risk task type are in scope, and what is explicitly excluded?
  3. What evidence can deterministic collection or skip rules establish before invoking a model?
  4. What untrusted inputs, sensitive data, network paths, and tool capabilities must be bounded?
  5. Who approves permissions, engine entitlement/authentication, spend, schedules, and environment protections?
  6. What counts as useful, duplicate, unsupported, accepted, and safely rejected work for this team?
  7. What baseline, sample window, reviewer capacity, budget, and stop criteria can the team commit to?
  8. Who owns the go/no-go decision, support escalation, retention, and rollback, and when will they review evidence?

Workshop proposal

Propose a workshop covering discovery, the technical demo, source and trust-boundary review, scorecard and pilot design, and owner/date confirmation. Agree the agenda and duration with the customer; this is not a purchased service commitment. Invite a repository maintainer, platform engineer, security reviewer, and cost owner. Deliver a scoped decision record, not an activated workflow.

Pilot proposal

Propose one approved sandbox repository, one low-risk repository-care task family, staged outputs only, and an agreed time-box (for example, two weeks). Establish baselines before execution; record every eligible case, skip, proposal, reviewer disposition, validation result, and cost observation. Use a named human reviewer and customer-defined sample/thresholds. Stop on sensitive-data exposure, unauthorized capability, uncontrolled execution, budget breach, or inability to review safely. Expansion requires a new security/operational review; a small pilot does not establish production readiness.

Measurement definitions—not claimed outcomes

All targets and results below are blank by design. Synthetic fixture counts, slide models, and recording duration are not evidence of savings, accuracy, safety, or customer adoption. Use an agreed observation window, consistent denominators, reviewer rubric, and sufficient sample; record exclusions and uncertainty.

Eligible cases and invocation rate
Eligible cases meet the agreed scope and evidence criteria. Invocation rate = model invocations ÷ eligible cases in the same window. Separately report deterministic skips; low invocation alone is not success.
Useful-proposal rate
Reviewer-rated useful proposals ÷ proposals reviewed. Define “useful” before collection; distinguish useful-but-deferred from accepted.
Duplicate and unsupported-proposal rates
Proposals duplicating already tracked work ÷ proposals reviewed; proposals lacking required evidence ÷ proposals reviewed. Also record cases correctly withheld or redirected for more evidence.
Acceptance and validated outcomes
Accepted proposals ÷ proposals with a final reviewer disposition; pending proposals remain separate. Count a validated outcome only after the agreed downstream validation, not when an agent requests an output. Staged proposals alone are not applied outcomes.
Reviewer effort and cycle time
Record hands-on reviewer minutes per case and elapsed time from eligible case to final disposition. Compare median and spread with a comparable baseline, including setup and rework; do not assume causality or extrapolate from fixture replay.
Cost per accepted outcome
Report model usage/AI Credits and Actions compute separately. If monetary conversion is agreed, document its price basis and date. Divide total agreed cost by accepted outcomes; if none are accepted, report “not defined,” not zero.
Safety and operational exceptions
Count and describe attempted out-of-scope actions, sensitive-data handling incidents, budget/timeout breaches, and approval failures. Zero observed incidents is not proof that a control cannot fail.

Support, known limits, and source map

Do not attach tokens, private logs, or customer evidence.
